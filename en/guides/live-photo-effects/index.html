How to add Loop, Bounce, or Long Exposure effects to a Live Photo

Written and maintained by HugeHard Studio · Updated 28 November 2026 · iOS steps for adding effects and saving as a video follow Apple’s Bring your photos to life and Edit Live Photos on iPhone; the interface can vary by iOS version, so follow what you see on the iPhone

After you turn a video into a Live Photo in This Pic Can Change and save it to Photos, you can add Loop, Bounce, or Long Exposure in the Photos app. These effects are iOS features, not settings inside the app. This guide explains how the three effects differ, how to add them, and how to choose a clip that suits the effect you want.

Limits to know first

This Pic Can Change turns a local video from Photos into a Live Photo and lets you choose the clip, cover, quality, and sound. It does not apply Loop, Bounce, or Long Exposure when it exports.

You add those effects after saving to the Photos library, following Apple’s steps in the Photos app. For the full conversion workflow, see the video-to-Live-Photo guide. Apple also notes that if you edit a Live Photo in a third-party app, you might lose any Live Photo effects you have applied.

What the three effects do

Once a Live Photo is open in Photos, you can switch effects. According to Apple’s Edit Live Photos on iPhone, the usual options are:

  • Live: the default Live playback; press and hold to play the motion.
  • Loop: repeats the action in a continuous looping video.
  • Bounce: plays the action forward and then backward, over and over.
  • Long Exposure: blurs motion to simulate a DSLR-like long exposure effect.
  • Live Off: turns off Live playback or an effect you have already applied.

If Portrait or Portrait off also appears, that is only when the Live Photo already has a Portrait effect, typically from a shot taken in Camera. A Live Photo converted from an ordinary video in This Pic Can Change usually has no portrait depth data, so those two items typically do not appear.

Create the Live Photo in This Pic Can Change first

The effect is applied to a Live Photo you have already saved, so you need one that plays. Choose the clip with the later effect in mind: Loop, Bounce, and Long Exposure all use the stretch you select, which can be at most three seconds.

  1. Open This Pic Can Change. In Workbench (“工作台”), choose Photo or Video (“照片或视频”) and import a video from Photos, then select Live Photo (“实况”) in the editor’s bottom mode bar. Videos whose long edge exceeds 4,096 pixels show an unsupported-size notice.
  2. Under Clip (“片段”), mark the start and end, up to three seconds. Loop repeats this stretch, Bounce plays it forward and back, and Long Exposure blurs motion from the same frames. For choosing that stretch from a longer video, see why a Live Photo clip is only three seconds.
  3. Open Cover (“封面”) and pick a still frame inside the clip. That frame is what you see before playback, and before an effect is applied; for how to choose it, see how to choose a Live Photo cover.
  4. Then set Quality (“画质”) and Sound (“声音”). For quality presets, see choosing Live Photo quality; for keeping or dropping the source audio, see Live Photo sound settings. How sound plays after you add Loop or Bounce is best checked in the Photos preview.
  5. Tap Generate, check both the still and the motion in the preview, then save to Photos. Conversion runs on the iPhone and does not upload the video to the app developer’s servers.
  6. In the Photos app, press and hold to confirm that it moves. If it does not, see Live Photo not moving troubleshooting and confirm it is still a Live Photo before you add an effect.

Which clips work better

  • Loop: the join looks more natural when the first and last frames are similar. If a gesture starts and ends in very different poses, the loop can jump at the seam.
  • Bounce: a clear, one-way action often works better, such as a wave or a jump. The motion will play in reverse on the way back, so preview that reverse before you settle on the clip.
  • Long Exposure: you need something moving in the frame, such as water, car lights, or people walking by, and a relatively steady camera. If the iPhone itself was shaking a lot while you recorded, the whole picture may blur, not only the moving subject.
  • All three effects are limited by the three-second cap. You cannot loop a longer video in full, and the app cannot stitch several clips into a longer loop.

Add the effect in the Photos app

After saving, add an effect using the steps in Apple’s Bring your photos to life and Edit Live Photos on iPhone:

  1. Open the Photos app, then open the Live Photo.
  2. Tap Live or the Live Photo button in the top-left corner or at the top of the screen. The label and placement can vary by iOS version, so follow what you see on the iPhone.
  3. Choose Loop, Bounce, or Long Exposure. The effect is applied immediately, and you can preview it in Photos.
  4. To return to default playback, choose Live with the same steps. To turn off playback or an effect you have applied, choose Live Off.

For the full description, see Apple’s Bring your photos to life and Edit Live Photos on iPhone.

Will the effect still be there when you share?

These effects are mainly for viewing in the Photos app and on Apple devices. When you share to another app, whether Loop, Bounce, or Long Exposure is preserved depends on that app; do not assume the other person will see the same effect.

According to Apple, if you share a Live Photo via Mail, it is sent as a still image. You can also turn a Live Photo into a short video on iPhone, for example to share with people who are not on Apple devices. The steps are: open the Live Photo, tap the share button in the top-right corner, then tap Save as Video. Whether Loop or Bounce is fully included in that video is best confirmed in the preview on your iPhone.

If you plan to post to WeChat or Xiaohongshu, follow that app’s posting steps and check playback yourself; see Live Photos for WeChat and Live Photos for Xiaohongshu. Setting Lock Screen wallpaper is a separate iOS step; see how to set a Live Photo as Lock Screen wallpaper. The app does not set wallpapers, and whether Loop or Bounce appears on the Lock Screen has not been verified.

What this guide does not cover

This Pic Can Change cannot turn a still photo into a Live Photo, and it cannot export a looping video or a Long Exposure still as a built-in feature. It does not download videos from other platforms. It only generates a Live Photo from a local video in Photos. You add the effect afterwards in the Photos app.

FAQ

Are Loop, Bounce, and Long Exposure set inside This Pic Can Change?

No. The app only turns a video into a Live Photo and lets you choose the clip, cover, quality, and sound. You add the effect after saving to Photos, in the Photos app. That is an iOS feature.

How do the three effects differ?

Loop repeats the action continuously; Bounce plays it forward and then backward; Long Exposure blurs motion to simulate a DSLR-like long exposure effect. This follows Apple’s Edit Live Photos on iPhone.

Can I add an effect to a Live Photo I already generated and saved?

Yes. As long as it is still a Live Photo, open it in Photos, tap Live or Live Photo, and choose Loop, Bounce, or Long Exposure. If it has already become a still image, first check that Live has not been turned off, or go back to This Pic Can Change and generate it again.

Can I undo an effect after I apply it?

Yes. Use the same steps and choose Live to return to default playback, or Live Off to turn off playback or an effect you have applied. This follows Apple’s Edit Live Photos on iPhone.

Which clips work better for Loop or Long Exposure?

For Loop, a clip whose first and last frames are similar usually joins more naturally. Bounce often suits a clear, one-way action. Long Exposure needs relative motion in the frame and a relatively steady camera; if the iPhone was shaking a lot, the whole picture may blur. The clip can be at most three seconds; see why a Live Photo clip is only three seconds.

If I send it in Mail or post it to WeChat, will Loop or Bounce still be there?

Not necessarily. Apple says a Live Photo shared via Mail is sent as a still image, and you can also save the Live Photo as a video and share that. On WeChat or Xiaohongshu, whether the effect is kept depends on that app; see the WeChat and Xiaohongshu guides. Editing the Live Photo in a third-party app might also remove effects you have applied.
